How big is your screen?

inches

Why do you ask?

Your browser knows how many pixels your screen has, but not how big it is in centimetres. With the size in inches we can work out the real scale. A 24" monitor and a 27" monitor can both be 1920 × 1080, so the pixels alone can't tell them apart.

Where to find it: the box, the sticker on the back or under the stand, the receipt, or the maker's spec page. Laptops: the model name is usually on the bottom. Curved screens: use the size the maker quotes.

Not sure? Measure with a bank card: every bank card is the same size (8.56 × 5.4 cm), so you can match one on screen and we take the scale from that.

How accurate is this?

Browsers cannot read the physical size of a screen, but they do report its resolution. With the size in inches that gives the scale exactly: pixels per inch is the diagonal in pixels divided by the diagonal in inches. The size is the one you typed, the one of the laptop model you picked, or the one you measured with a bank card (Fine-tune), which is the most direct.

If the screen's resolution matches a known laptop, the page suggests that model and fills in its size; for monitors and anything else it never guesses a size. Browser zoom and display scaling change what the browser reports, so for a model the page warns when it looks off. Check with a bank card when in doubt.
