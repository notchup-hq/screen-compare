What is this?

Thinking of a new laptop, phone or monitor? Screen compare draws it at its real size on the screen you have now, so you can see the difference before you buy. For example, on a 16" laptop it shows exactly how much smaller a 14" MacBook Pro would be. It works the other way too: a bigger screen shows how far it would reach past your edges. Why do you ask?

Your browser knows how many pixels your screen has, but not how big it is in centimetres. With the size in inches we can work out the real scale. A 24" monitor and a 27" monitor can both be 1920 × 1080, so the pixels alone can't tell them apart.

Where to find it: the box, the sticker on the back or under the stand, the receipt, or the maker's spec page. Laptops: the model name is usually on the bottom. Phones and tablets: Settings, then About, or the maker's spec page. Curved screens: use the size the maker quotes.

Not sure? Measure with a card. Bank, ID, driving licence, loyalty and library cards are all the same size (8.56 × 5.4 cm), so you can match one on screen and we take the scale from that.

    How accurate is this?

    Browsers cannot read the physical size of a screen, but they do report its resolution. With the size in inches that gives the scale exactly: pixels per inch is the diagonal in pixels divided by the diagonal in inches. The size is the one you typed, the one of the laptop model you picked, or the one you measured with a card (Fine-tune), which is the most direct.

    If the screen's resolution matches a known laptop, the page suggests that model and fills in its size; for monitors and anything else it never guesses a size. Browser zoom and display scaling change what the browser reports, so for a model the page warns when it looks off. Check with a card when in doubt. It all runs in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.
